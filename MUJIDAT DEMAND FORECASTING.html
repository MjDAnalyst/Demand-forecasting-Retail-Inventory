Case Study: Demand Forecasting for Retail Inventory

In [ ]:
  1. Business Problem A mid-sized retail chain operating across multiple regions struggles with inventory mismanagement — some products frequently run out of stock (resulting in lost sales), while others sit unsold for long periods, tying up cash and increasing storage costs. Leadership wants a data-driven approach to forecasting demand so that inventory and promotional decisions can be made proactively rather than reactively. Stakeholders: Retail owners, supply chain managers, finance teams Decisions to be informed: • How much stock to reorder, and when • When to time promotions for maximum impact without overstock risk
  2. Business Objectives • Reduce stockouts (lost revenue, poor customer experience) • Reduce overstocking (excess holding costs, markdowns) • Improve cash flow through better inventory turnover

#evaluation metric

def mape(y_true, y_pred):
    y_true, y_pred = np.array(y_true), np.array(y_pred)
    mask = y_true != 0  # avoid divide-by-zero on zero-sales days
    return np.mean(np.abs((y_true[mask] - y_pred[mask]) / y_true[mask])) * 100

def evaluate(name, y_true, y_pred):
    mae = mean_absolute_error(y_true, y_pred)
    rmse = np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_true, y_pred))
    m = mape(y_true, y_pred)
    print(f"{name:35s} MAE: {mae:7.2f}   RMSE: {rmse:7.2f}   MAPE: {m:7.2f}%")
    return mae, rmse, m

KEY INSIGHTS

  1. Neither the rolling-mean baseline nor the Random Forest model meaningfully outperforms a naive overall mean forecast — MAE stays ~89-93 units and MAPE ~345-348% regardless of model complexity.
  2. This is consistent with our earlier EDA finding that Units Sold behaves as near random noise in this dataset, with no learnable signal from category, region, season, weather, or promotion features.
  3. Price, recent lags, and rolling averages are the most 'important' features to the Random Forest, but none carry enough signal to meaningfully reduce error, reinforcing that the ceiling on forecast accuracy here is set by the data generating process, not by model choice.
  4. The dataset's own 'Demand Forecast' column outperforms both models by a wide margin, but investigation shows it's derived directly from actual Units Sold plus noise, making it an unfair/leaky benchmark rather than a genuine forecasting target to beat.
